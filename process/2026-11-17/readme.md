# Presentation

## Title of Your Project

Echo: From Play to Prevention

## Your names

Lorie CRETTEX, Nouria DA ROS

## Pitch

Echo is a choice-driven narrative game about cyberbullying, for ages 12–16. Echo, the first hologram child of the Holo’Kids program, joins class 3B at Alan Turing middle school.

Called “fake” from day one, Echo becomes the class’s target. The player embodies a classmate whose choices make them a witness, an accomplice or an ally.

Keywords: identity, body, boundaries, nomophobia.

## What is the experience about

**The player**

The player must choose between following the group, staying quiet or defending Echo at the risk of becoming the next target (“If you say nothing, you agree”).

**Echo**

Echo goes through an identity crisis and faces a dilemma: accept the labels stuck on them (“fake”, “you’re not human”, “you have no parents”), or decide who they are.

**Clarisse**

Clarisse, mocked for her family’s poverty, harasses Echo to make a place for herself in La Serre. She is both victim and harasser.

**Kovasko**

Kovasko, the teacher, notices nothing: it is the player who decides, in the game, whether to tell him.

The story shows the central mechanisms of bullying: exclusion by the group (“Humans of 3B only”), the search for a scapegoat, pressure on witnesses and dehumanization (“it’s just a machine”).

It also shows that bullying is passed on: Clarisse makes Echo go through what she lived. And if the bullying goes too far, Echo cannot take it anymore and short-circuits.

Everything happens fast, in public and out of the adults’ sight. The class is silent, but laughter bursts out on the phones. In one night, the video, the meme and the exclusion group spread (0:12, 0:30, 1:05), and Echo’s message to a student in distress is rejected within minutes (2:40, 2:45).

Students are added by force to the private group and pushed to stay. Echo’s system is hacked to display insults behind their back.

Echo being a hologram makes one aspect of online bullying concrete: when the target doesn’t seem “real”, hurting them seems to have no consequences.

## What is understood / What knowledge is learned

- **Tools adolescents can learn**
- Recognize the three roles (witness, accomplice, ally), and understand that silence and a “like” are choices too.
- Concrete actions: don’t like or share, leave the group, erase the insults, warn the targeted person, take screenshots and talk to a trusted adult.
- Read other people’s emotions, thanks to Echo’s flowers that open or close.
- Understand that the person who bullies can suffer too, and that a victim can become a harasser (Clarisse).

**How the story leads to this understanding**

Choices made in the moment have consequences later, as in real life. Echo’s state gauge makes the effects of bullying visible without any label: a face goes from happy to angry and the bar from green to orange to red. The more it increases, the more Echo degrades (bugs, seizure-like crises), until Echo short-circuits. The closer the player gets to Echo, the more their story is revealed: knowing someone makes that person human in our eyes.

The three endings contrast what happens when nobody reacts (Echo is switched off and never comes back to 3B) and when someone reacts (the plan fails, doubt sets in for Clarisse and Echo stays).

## Agency

The player acts in every scene, and small everyday gestures (laughing, liking, staying quiet) carry weight. Nobody saves Echo alone: in the final scene, the outcome depends on the choices of the witness, Kovasko and Clarisse. A single person reacting is enough to make the plan fail.

## How is your experience played?

A workshop in class led by a teacher or a facilitator. Like Netflix games: the story plays out on a computer, and each student connects their phone or iPad to play on their own. On their device, each one receives the class group’s messages, notifications and memes, and can react to them (like, comment, leave the group, take a screenshot, warn Echo). Their reactions then influence the story. At the end, each one receives a title-role based on the choices they made, and everyone talks about it afterwards.

## The playable prototype (new)

The prototype is no longer built in Unity: it is a web page, playable in a browser (`code/index.html`).

- **Scenes like Street View.** Each place is a full-screen image, and the player walks from one place to the next by clicking arrows: the hallway, class 3B, the roof, La Serre and Altaya. A pink arrow guides the player to the next scene of the story.
- **Altaya stays hidden at the start.** The arrow to Altaya only appears if the player has become friends with Echo (the bond is strong enough). Otherwise, Echo investigates alone, and that part of the story stays closed.
- **Visual novel dialogue.** The story and the choices appear in a text box at the bottom of the scene, with a title page, an introduction, and the choice of a name and an avatar before the first scene.
- **Echo’s state gauge** shows a face and a green-orange-red bar, with no written label.
- **The journal** is already written: it fills in scene after scene with the player’s own doubts (“I wonder if it’s okay that we make fun of Echo”).
- Nine scenes so far, from “The arrival” to “Friday, after class”, in French and English.

## Questions for the cool kids

- Have you ever seen something in a class group without saying anything? What held you back?
- What would make it easier to leave a group or report a message?
- Who would you go to for help: a teacher, a parent, a friend?
- Does Echo feel like a real classmate to you, even as a hologram?
- Are the choices realistic? Is there a reaction you would have had that is missing?
- Do the characters’ messages and way of speaking look like what you see on your phone?
- Would you rather play alone or with friends? On a phone or on a computer?

## Universe

**Holo’Kids program, coming soon to your class.** Echo is the first hologram child of the Holo’Kids program, developed by Altaya, an experimental research institute on new technologies. Holo’Kids are integrated into schools as teaching tools: they support students in difficulty and strengthen solidarity within the class.

**Echo’s emotional system.** Echo expresses their emotions through flowers that open or close. This visual signal lets the player read what Echo feels without Echo having to say it.

**Core mechanic.** Each choice quietly changes the bond between the player and Echo. A strong enough bond unlocks access to Echo’s story (their lore) later in the game. Without getting closer, that part of the story stays closed.

## Walkthrough

**The arrival.**

First day, Echo is the only holokid in class 3B. Kovasko gives a speech on diversity, then Echo introduces themself out loud, while no one speaks in class anymore.

- *React →* look with disdain, smile, no reaction

To say hello, Echo reaches out to a boy, and their hands cross. The unease is immediate, and the flowers open on their own. Silence, then laughter. A student films.

- *React:* laugh with the class → yes/no

**0:12:** the video is posted on the 3B group, in the class group (“you’re not even real”, “fake”, “your emotions are fictions”).

**0:30:** a meme of Echo being walked through on a loop, captioned (get out of here u’re not even real).

- *Reaction →* like the meme, don’t react, write that it’s mean

**1:05:** a group “Humans of 3B only” is created, without Echo.

**La Serre.**

The next day, the class is silent, but laughter bursts out on their phones. Kovasko notices nothing.

- *Reaction →* talk to Mr. Kovasko, do nothing, laugh with the others

At break, Echo follows the others to the roof. In front of La Serre’s door, a student blocks the way: “It’s reserved for the real ones.” Echo tries to enter anyway.

- *Reactions →* open it for them, do nothing, hack La Serre’s door to create a trap that electrocutes Echo and take a photo

Clarisse isn’t in La Serre either: mocked for her poverty, she harasses Echo to try to make a place for herself there.

**The labels.**

The students manage to hack Echo’s system to write insults behind their back.

- *Reaction →* write an insult, warn Echo, don’t react, remove the insults

Clarisse calls Echo a “life thief”: she explains that it’s because of people like them that her family became poor and that she gets mocked. The paradox: Echo is kind and just wants to help.

**2:40:** in the WhatsApp group, a student writes that he is not okay. Echo is the only one to answer.

The answer is rejected: “You can’t understand, you’re not human.” Echo is hurt.

**“Turn it off”.**

Clarisse creates a private group: “We’re not harassing anyone, it’s just a machine.” Students are added by force and forced to stay (“Are you with us or with the machine?”, “If you say nothing, you agree”). Fear of being the next victim is everywhere.

There is a shutdown code, **KIPE-SAFE007**. The plan: Friday, after class, switch Echo off and film the scene. Clarisse shares her plan in La Serre: for the first time, the popular students give her access. The witness understands: Echo has become the new scapegoat.

- *The witness (player) chooses now, and the consequences come later:* take part, stay quiet, leave the group, send screenshots to Kovasko, warn Echo.

**The origins.**

“You have no parents” haunts Echo. Echo investigates, alone, or with the witness if the player warned them in step 4. Memory fragments, archives, a trail to Altaya. Altaya is a small team that created the Holokids program for isolated children, unable to express their feelings. … Echo finally understands who they are and what they are for.

**Friday, after class.**

The meeting is set on the roof, in front of La Serre. Echo now knows their identity. The class empties, the group waits, someone takes out their phone to film. If the player warned Kovasko or Echo earlier, the scene changes.

- *The witness:* film, stay quiet, leave, contradict the group in front of everyone, or open the door to Echo.
- What Kovasko, Clarisse and Echo do depends on your earlier choices.

**Ending.**

Echo is covered in labels, but the flowers they were mocked for at the beginning become proof that Echo feels things. Echo doesn’t enter La Serre. “No one has the power to define who you are.”

## Endings

A gauge, shown only as a face and a green-to-red bar, tracks what the player puts Echo through.

- **If you harass:** go too far and Echo short-circuits: overwhelmed by what they go through, Echo shuts down.
- **If you stay passive:** the plan to turn Echo off goes ahead: Echo is switched off against the glass door and the video is displayed in La Serre. The code sends a signal to Altaya, who restarts Echo a few days later. But Echo doesn’t come back to 3B, and their seat stays empty.
- **If you help Echo:** the plan fails. You can open the door to Echo, even if the group rejects you. Clarisse realizes there was no machine to fight, only the group, and doubt sets in. Echo stays. You go through Echo’s family story with them, and things get better in class: no more bullying, no to cyberbullying.
