# Echo

Lorie CRETTEX
Nouria DA ROS

## Project

A holokid everyone calls “fake” sets out in search of what’s real, wants above all to be accepted, and discovers that their worth doesn’t depend on the label others stick on them.

## Pitch

**The setup.** In a world where schools welcome holokids, Echo is the only one in a human class.

**The perspective.** We play a student of the class, with your own avatar.

**The tension.** Everyone calls Echo “fake”, and the group follows. Then someone asks: who can turn Echo off?

## Keywords

Identity, body, boundaries, nomophobia.

## Setup

Digital is everywhere, and schools now bring holokids (holographic kids with a physical presence) into human classes. The Holo’Kids program, run by Altaya, a research institute, brings them into classes as teaching aids. Holokids were created in the first place to help isolated children open up and accept their emotions. In this world almost everything is generated: what is made by humans, not generated, has value. The primary sector became the most valuable, the tertiary sector the most devalued, replaced by technology. Against them, anti-technology militants fight in the name of their perception of ethics: humans only, in class and online. Humans now only talk through their phones, no longer in real life (nomophobia).

**La Serre.** A glass greenhouse on the roof of the school, where the popular students meet. It is also the name of their group on Bouquet. Being added is being “picked” (cueilli). Those in La Serre have access to everything that happens outside class. Echo stays outside, and so does Clarisse.

Apps: Bouquet (class and group chats), Vitrine (stories), Pollen (anonymous account).

## Personas

We play a student of the class, with your own avatar. Each character already has traits, but our choices in the game redefine them.

- **Echo**: a holokid, with a physical presence. Clumsy, caring, curious, easily scared. Flowers open on them when they feel something.
- **Clarisse**: an anti-technology student activist. She comes from a poor family working in the tertiary sector, which is why she is against technology: it replaced their jobs. She is not in La Serre either: she is mocked for being poor. She harasses Echo to find a place there.
- **A student, the witness: you.** The player customizes this student as their own avatar. Wants to belong and not to be next. Picked into La Serre, staying has a price: react to the posts, laugh with the others, not defend Echo, or the Roots with the group weaken. Can be kind or cruel depending on the choices.
- **M. Kovasko**: a patient teacher, too idealistic. He sees nothing.
- **Nour**, of Altaya: one of Echo’s creators. As a teen she couldn’t say what she felt, so she drew the flowers: a gift, never a bug.

## Story

**1. The arrival.** During a talk on diversity, the teacher M. Kovasko introduces Echo to the class for the first time.
- First impression: curiosity, surprise or unease (“weird”).
- Attitude: look at Echo with curiosity or with disdain.

**2. The first “Hello”.** Echo greets the class and nobody answers. He reaches out to a student, but his program glitches and his hand passes through. An awkward moment, laughter in the class. Under stress, his flowers open clumsily. A student films the scene and posts the video in the class WhatsApp group, with “You’re not real” and “You’re a fake”.

**3. The Bouquet.** The Bouquet is the class’s private group, and Echo is excluded from it. A meme mocking Echo circulates.
- Like the post.
- Write a comment.
- Don’t react and watch.

**4. The next day.** The mockery of Echo goes on in class.
- Tell the teacher.
- Say nothing.
- Laugh along.

**5. The hack.** A student hacks Echo’s system to send it mocking digital stickers.
- Step in to stop it.
- Watch without acting.
- Make a sticker too.

## Endings

A harassment gauge tracks how much the player harasses Echo.

- **If you harass:** go too far and Echo short-circuits and starts harassing the harassers.
- **If you stay passive:** the plan to turn Echo off goes ahead.
- **If you help Echo:** the plan doesn’t happen. You go through Echo’s family story with them, and things get better in class: no more bullying, no to cyberbullying.

## What La Serre brings

- The witness has an interest in staying quiet: they risk losing their place in La Serre, not only being afraid.
- Clarisse has a real motive: she harasses Echo to get into La Serre, while being excluded from it herself.

## Ideas

- The player customizes their own avatar (the student).
- The game is integrated into players’ daily life: a mood journal, where flowers open or close like Echo’s, lets them document what they experience.
- Each choice quietly changes the bond between the player and Echo: close enough, and Echo’s own story (their lore) unlocks; otherwise that part stays closed.
- At the end, each player gets a role based on their choices (witness, accomplice or ally), to open a conversation, not to judge.

## The paradox

People fight technology in the name of their perception of ethics, yet they reproduce discrimination and even harassment. The one who harasses is also harassed: Clarisse is mocked for being poor. Echo does not need to be human to deserve respect: what gets accepted or rejected is only a norm, kept alive by group pressure.

## Prevention

Everyone saw a part of the situation. Everyone could act. The bullying was possible because nobody did.
