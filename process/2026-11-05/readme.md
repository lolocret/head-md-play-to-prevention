# Echo

Lorie CRETTEX
Nouria DA ROS

## Project

A technological holographic kid everyone calls “fake” sets out in search of what’s real, and discovers that their worth doesn’t depend on the label others stick on them.

## Pitch

**The setup.** In a world where schools welcome technological holographic kids, Echo is the only Kidya (a technological holographic kid) in a human class.

**The perspective.** We follow three people: Echo, a student who watches, and M. Kovasko, a teacher who sees nothing.

**The tension.** Everyone calls Echo “fake”, and the group follows. Then someone asks: who can turn Echo off?

## Keywords

Identity, body, boundaries, homophobia.

## Setup

Digital people are everywhere, and schools now bring Kidya (technological holographic kids with a physical presence) into human classes. Against them, anti-technology militants fight in the name of ethical values: humans only, in class and online.

## Personas

Each already has character traits, but our choices in the game redefine them.

- **Echo**: a Kidya, a technological holographic kid with a physical presence. Clumsy, caring, curious, easily scared. Flowers open on them when they feel something.
- **Clarisse**: an anti-technology student activist. She comes from a poor family working in the tertiary sector, which is why she is against technology: it replaced their jobs. She harasses Echo, but she is mocked herself for being poor.
- **A student**: the witness. Wants to belong and not to be next. The player customizes this student as their own avatar. Can be kind or cruel depending on our choices: take part, stay quiet, leave the group, send screenshots to the teacher, or warn Echo.
- **M. Kovasko**: a patient teacher, too idealistic.
- **The director of Altaya**: Echo’s creator. The program’s original goal is to help children stop isolating and accept their emotions.

## Story

**1. The arrival.** First day, Echo is the only Kidya in the class. Kovasko gives a speech on diversity, then Echo introduces themself. To say hello, Echo reaches out to a student, and their hand passes right through the student’s. The awkwardness is instant, and the flowers open on their own. Silence, then laughter. A student films. 8:12 pm: the video is posted in the class chat (“you’re not even real”, “fake”, “your emotions are fictions”). 8:30 pm: memes, flowers on a loop, a “FAKE” sticker. 9:05 pm: a group “3B humains only”, without Echo. Next day: students laughing on their phones, Kovasko sees nothing.

**2. The labels.** Insults turn into stickers stuck on Echo. Clarisse’s group accuses Echo of privilege. The paradox: Echo is clumsy and alone, not perfect. 10:40 pm: a student writes that they are not doing well, and Echo is the only one to answer (the player picks the advice); the answer is thrown back: “you can’t relate, you’re not human”. Flowers closed, Echo hurt. 11:58 pm: an anonymous account without a photo posts a poll, “who can turn it off?”, then “you have no parents”. Seen by 24 people, nobody answers.

**3. “Turn it off”.** Clarisse creates a private group: “We don’t harass anyone. We turn off a machine.” Students are added by force and pressured to stay (“are you with us or with the machine?”, “if you say nothing, you agree”). Fear of being next. The anonymous account is added to the group, but by whom? A shutdown code, KIPE-SAFE007, and the time Echo will be alone. The plan: switch Echo off after class and film it.
- *A student:* take part, stay quiet, leave, send screenshots to the teacher, warn Echo.
- *Kovasko:* whispers and Echo isolated, step in or wait.
- *Echo:* avoided by everyone, run away, talk to an adult, or stay quiet.
- Nobody acts: Echo is switched off in front of the class, and the video circulates. Someone acts: the plan fails and Clarisse doubts.

**4. The revelations.** “You have no parents” haunts Echo. Investigation, alone or with an ally depending on the choices. Memory fragments, erased archives, a trail to the Altaya institute. Echo is a human-AI hybrid, a secret experiment. Their parents: the director of Altaya and a non-binary person, a sex worker who was exploited. The protocol notes contain “who can turn it off” and “your emotions are fictions”, word for word. The anonymous account is the director: Echo’s own parent led the bullying, as a test to measure their emotions. Echo is hurt. Clarisse was used by Altaya while believing she was fighting technology. She realizes that Echo was never the problem: Altaya is. Her anger changes target.

**5. The fire.** Clarisse’s anger changes target: her group plans to burn Altaya down. Echo, hurt, goes in to find the archives. The fire starts.
- *Clarisse:* let it burn, or save Echo.
- *Kovasko:* stay a spectator, or finally act.
- *Echo:* save the proof of their humanity, or let it burn.
Flowers open in the middle of the fire.

**Ending.** Echo is covered in stickers and chooses which to keep and which to tear off. The flowers they were mocked for at the beginning become proof that Echo feels. “No one has the power to certify who you are.”

## Ideas

- The player customizes their own avatar (the student).
- The game is integrated into players’ daily life: a mood journal, where flowers open or close like Echo’s, lets them document what they experience.
- At the end, the player is given a role, based on the choices they made.

## The paradox

People fight technology in the name of ethical values, yet they reproduce discrimination and even harassment. The one who harasses is also harassed: Clarisse is mocked for being poor. Once we understand that Echo is real, even a human-AI hybrid, it questions what gets accepted or rejected according to norms and group pressure.

## Prevention

Everyone saw a part of the situation. Everyone could act. The bullying was possible because nobody did.
